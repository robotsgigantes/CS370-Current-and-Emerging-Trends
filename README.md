What do computer scientists do and why does it matter?  
Computer scientists solve problems. To solve these problems, they use technology in innovative ways to approach, process, and complete complex tasks to save energy, time, and money. The reason this matters is that it benefits not only the scientist, but also humanity and the sciences. For example, neural networks were created to help machines learn. Through analyzing how the brain works, computer scientists were able to recreate neurons that computers could use to learn and process information in the same way as humans. By doing this, we as a society have been able to have automated and catered experiences in our day to day lives, as well as further our understanding of how both humans and machines learn.

How do I approach a problem as a computer scientist?  
The way that I approach a problem as a computer scientist is how I approach problems not within the CS realm. I look at the big picture, and dissect smaller, more manageable, problems from it. Being able to complete smaller tasks that benefit the larger problem helps to make an easier and more logical solution to that problem. Similarly, this saves time and energy from trying to manage the solution to the problem as a whole.

What are my ethical responsibilities to the end user and the organization?
